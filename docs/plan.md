# Sur ki Neev: Riyaz Together
### The final recommended approach for your son (age 4)

This replaces the earlier "Riyaz ki Shuruaat" plan. The app can keep that name. The plan still follows the order a guru would teach in, but your son starts every step from things he already does well: singing whole rhymes, echoing, and finishing lines.

**A few words you'll see**
- **Sa**: the home note. Every Indian melody comes back to it.
- **Re, Ga, Ma, Pa, Dha, Ni**: the other notes.
- **Sargam**: singing the note names.
- **Swar**: a note.
- **Tanpura (drone)**: the steady humming background that keeps Sa sounding.
- **Aakar**: singing on "aa".
- **Taal**: a rhythm cycle.
  - Keherwa has 8 beats, Dadra 6 and Teentaal 16.
  - **Sam** is beat 1, the "home" beat.
  - **Taali** is a clap. In this plan it starts as a quiet clap: two fingers on your palm.
  - **Khali** is the "empty" beat, shown with a wave.
- **Bols / padhant**: tabla syllables ("Dha Ge Na Ti") spoken in rhythm. This is the traditional way to keep taal.
- **Theka**: the tabla pattern for a taal.
- **Alankar**: a short practice pattern of notes.
- **Aaroh / avaroh**: going up / coming down.
- **Sargam geet (swarmalika)**: a short song sung only in note names.
- **Raag Bhoopali**: a five-note raag (Sa Re Ga Pa Dha), usually the first raag a child learns.
- **ABGMVM**: Akhil Bharatiya Gandharva Mahavidyalaya Mandal, a national board that runs Gandharva music exams through local centres. Other boards and schools publish their own rules.
- **Cents**: a tuning measure. 100 cents is one harmonium key. "±75 cents" means "within about three-quarters of a key".
- **Note heights used here**: your son's Sa is set to D4 by default (293.66 Hz, harmonium key "safed 2"). Then Re = E4, Ga = F#4, Pa = A4 and Dha = B4.

## 1. What we learned

**1. This is music learning, not autism therapy.**
- **What the research found:**
  - The 2022 Cochrane review found that therapist-led music therapy probably helps overall improvement in the short term. It showed no clear effect on social interaction or on communication, and certainty there is low to very low.
  - The largest trial, TIME-A, studied 364 children aged 4 to 7, the same age as your son. Music therapy did no better than ordinary care, and three sessions a week did no better than one.
- **What it means for us:**
  - Aim for real musical skill and shared enjoyment.
  - Don't expect speech gains from this.
  - A skipped day costs nothing, and longer sessions won't do more.
- **Evidence:** strong (for "don't expect therapy gains").
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD004381_music-therapy-autistic-people
  - https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5817481

**2. You do not need to be a singer.**
- **What the research found:**
  - In the UK IMAGINE: Autism project (11 families, then 25), parents with no musical training used simple structured music activities. The researchers concluded that "specialist musical training is not a requirement". Singing was the activity families used most and kept up longest.
  - A small Melbourne trial of family music therapy at home (23 children aged 3 to 5) improved how parent and child engaged with each other. It did not improve language.
  - What parents valued, and kept doing, was enjoying it together.
  - A coached home music programme (MAP) was well liked, but only about 1 in 5 families completed it.
- **What it means for us:** you are a partner, not a performer. Plan for your worst day, not your best one.
- **Evidence:** moderate. The studies are small and mostly parent-reported.
- **Sources:**
  - https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/20592043211017362
  - https://researchonline.rcm.ac.uk/id/eprint/2445
  - https://findanexpert.unimelb.edu.au/scholarlywork/603197-family-centred-music-therapy-to-promote-social-engagement-in-young-children-with-severe-autism-spectrum-disorder--a-randomized-controlled-study
  - https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/32438/1/Williams%20et%20al%202024_MAP%20feasibiity%20RCT.pdf

**3. Copy him first.**
- **What the research found:** in small studies of autistic children aged about 3 to 5, adults copied each sound the child made. The children then made more sounds, took more turns and copied more. Parents learned to do this quickly.
- **What it means for us:** every session starts with him leading and you copying. Only after that do you offer a phrase for him to copy. This builds on his echoing instead of asking him to perform.
- **Evidence:** moderate. The studies are small but have been repeated.
- **Sources:**
  - https://behavioristbookclub.com/aba-research/autism-developmental/contingent-imitation-increases-verbal-interaction-in-children-with-aut/
  - https://ifp.nyu.edu/2026/journal-article-abstracts/bin-70083/

**4. Pause just before the note he knows.**
- **What the research found:**
  - Therapist manuals advise stopping just before a predictable word and waiting about 3 to 5 seconds. Any sound counts.
  - In a 1991 study, parents of three autistic boys paused expectantly during daily routines, and the boys' spontaneous speech increased.
  - A mother describes her echolalic 3-year-old calling out the missing word when she paused in a song.
  - No study has tested this inside songs specifically.
- **What it means for us:** "Finish the line" is a core game. He finishes a familiar sargam line, and if he stays quiet the music simply carries on.
- **Evidence:** moderate for pausing in general; based on practice experience for songs.
- **Sources:**
  - https://talk.ku.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/PC-TALK-Manual-34.pdf
  - https://behavioristbookclub.com/aba-research/autism-developmental/increasing-autistic-children-s-daily-spontaneous-speech/
  - https://grownandflown.com/connecting-autistic-son-through-music/

**5. Start from rhymes. Holding Sa is a warm-up, not a gate.**
- **What the research found:**
  - Indian classes for under-6s (Shankar Mahadevan Academy (SMA) "Rhymes to Swaras", Little Ustaads) begin with welcome songs, clapping, high/low games and rhymes sung with their notes. They do not begin with holding Sa.
  - In studies of young children, holding one note was not reliably the easiest task.
  - Children sing more in tune when they sing along with someone. In-tune singing of a whole phrase often arrives only around age 5½ to 6.
  - Your son already sings whole songs. That is the top early-years level in the Sounds of Intent framework.
- **What it means for us:** Sa practice stays every day, but nothing waits for it.
- **Evidence:** moderate. These are course descriptions and studies of school-age children.
- **Sources:**
  - https://shankarmahadevanacademy.com/learn-rhymes-to-swaras-teacher-led/RTS101TL
  - https://journals.library.mun.ca/index.php/singing/article/download/945/820/3266
  - https://digital.lib.washington.edu/researchworks/items/e2fb6b1f-331a-451b-a752-de05d7183020/full
  - https://nurseryworld.co.uk/articles/learning-development-music-with-intent

**6. A real singing voice is a better model than the harmonium, and note names should always be sung, never spoken.**
- **What the research found:**
  - Children matched pitch better from a voice than from a piano. They did best with a child's voice, then a woman's, then a man's. These were school-age children.
  - Speech therapists widely describe echolalic children storing whole phrases together with their tune. The framework behind this is unproven, but the precaution costs nothing.
  - One mother found her son coped with plain, unornamented singing before he could cope with expressive singing.
- **What it means for us:**
  - Record one real singer now. Don't wait for a teacher.
  - Your son must never hear "Sa Re Ga" in a talking voice, because he may store it as speech.
- **Evidence:** weak to moderate. Treat this as a low-risk precaution.
- **Sources:**
  - https://pel.psu.edu/files/2020/07/Nichols-2020-PoM-1.pdf
  - https://repository.lsu.edu/gradschool_disstheses/4357
  - https://www.hanen.org/information-tips/3-things-you-should-know-about-echolalia
  - https://guernicamag.com/?p=83783

**7. His voice: a small range, shape before exact pitch, and one fixed Sa.**
- **What the research found:**
  - A comfortable range for 3- to 5-year-olds is about D4 to A4. With Sa at D4, that is Sa up to Pa.
  - In one study, autistic participants were weaker at matching exact pitch but typical at matching the shape of a melody (up, down, same).
  - Perfect pitch is reported more often in autism (estimates of 5 to 11 percent, from small samples). He may remember exact pitches.
- **What it means for us:**
  - Measure his Sa from his own singing, then never change it casually for anything he already knows.
  - Much later, let him meet new pieces at a second Sa, slowly, so that a class or an exam can work (Stage 3 onward).
  - Reward the shape of a phrase first, and start with wide tolerance.
  - Keep everything between Sa and Pa for now.
- **Evidence:** moderate. The main study was of older, Mandarin-speaking participants, and no study has tested autistic 4-year-olds.
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.buffalo.edu/news/releases/2021/08/006.html
  - https://ubwp.buffalo.edu/apal-pfordresher/wp-content/uploads/sites/79/2022/01/Wang_etal_inpress_AutRes.pdf
  - https://socialsci.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Early_Childhood_Education/General_Child_Development/Music_and_the_Child_(Sarrazin)/01%3A_Chapters/1.05%3A_Children_Singing_and_Childrens_Songs
  - https://www.pubmed.gov/34821355/

**8. Loud sound needs care, and his over-excitement may partly be him seeking big sensations.**
- **What the research found:**
  - Parents report reduced sound tolerance in about 78 percent of autistic children. Measured hyperacusis is about 27 percent. Loud and sudden sounds are common triggers.
  - A case report warns that relying on noise-cancelling headphones long-term may increase sensitivity.
  - A child can both seek and avoid sensations. An occupational therapist's (OT's) sensory profile can tell which is which.
  - Children react very differently to the same sounds and the same raags.
- **What it means for us:**
  - Soft starts and a volume ceiling the tablet really keeps to.
  - No celebration sounds, and quiet claps from you.
  - A Calm button, a clear response when a sound upsets him, and his own "all done" card to stop.
  - Calm options tested on him, not assumed.
  - A hearing test and sound-tolerance check by an audiologist, as well as an OT's sensory profile.
- **Evidence:** moderate for how common sound sensitivity is; weak for any specific calming method.
- **Sources:**
  - https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8349927
  - https://www.pubmed.gov/34524587/
  - https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2024.1440624/pdf
  - https://understood.org/articles/understanding-sensory-processing-issues

**9. Gandharva Prarambhik is realistically around age 7, and there are options before that.**
- **What the research found:**
  - The Pune Gandharva Mahavidyalaya's own site gives 7 as the minimum age for its Prarambhik exam. For ages 4 to 7 it offers "Balgeet", a song-and-game class. Its own pages disagree on whether the lower age is 4 or "more than 5".
  - Gandharva exams are run by more than one body. ABGMVM runs exams through local centres, and the Pune school publishes its own rules. Which age rule applies depends on your centre.
  - The New Delhi Gandharva Mahavidyalaya is a separate institution with much older minimum ages; about 11 in a recent year.
  - Prarambhik is sung with tabla. It covers the aaroh-avaroh of Bhoopali and Yaman (Bilawal on some boards), alankars at slow and medium speed, and Teentaal, Dadra and Keherwa.
  - Options exist now, though how well they suit an autistic child is unknown:
    - SMA Rhymes to Swaras (ages 2.5 to 6, at most 3 children per online batch)
    - Hitham Trust (Chennai)
    - The Music Therapy Trust (Delhi)
    - SMA's course for special children
- **What it means for us:**
  - Aim for a one-to-one teacher trial around age 5, then Balgeet-style singing, then Prarambhik at around 7, once your local centre confirms its current age rule.
  - Avoid noisy rooms where many students practise at once.
  - A group class sings at one shared Sa, and an exam accompanist may not use his, so he will need to get used to other Sa's slowly (Stage 3 onward).
- **Evidence:** strong for what the Pune school's own pages say. Whether your local exam centre uses the same age rule still needs checking.
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.gandharvapune.org/faqs/
  - https://www.gandharvapune.org/balgeet/
  - https://gandharvamahavidyalayanewdelhi.org/?p=244
  - https://abgmvm.org/?p=699

**10. The current Level 1 test app sometimes rewards the wrong thing, and its volume limit isn't a real limit.**
- **What the research found:**
  - It adds up in-tune moments across the whole 9-second turn, and it counts Sa in any octave. So simply singing a rhyme earned "found Sa" in roughly 17 to 50 percent of simulated "Easy" turns.
  - It folds pitch into one octave, so Pa, Dha and Ni appear *below* the Sa line.
  - It accepts sounds from 70 to 1100 Hz, which lets a man's voice or fan hum count.
  - It shows him text prompts and try counts.
  - Its volume limit only scales the app's own sound (`applyVol`, line 213, set by the slider at line 149). The tablet's own media volume, which YouTube shares, sits on top of it. If YouTube was loud just before, or the volume keys get pressed, the app can play above the limit.
- **What it means for us:** fix these before trusting the app's log, and make the volume limit a real one. Section 9 lists every fix, in the order each is needed.
- **Evidence:** strong for the code facts, which were checked line by line. The percentages come from a simulation and are only an illustration.
- **Sources:** [index.html](../index.html); [.github/workflows/apk.yml](../.github/workflows/apk.yml)

## 2. The approach in one paragraph

We keep the order a guru would teach in:
1. Sa against the tanpura.
2. Sa-Pa-Sa.
3. The first few notes (Sa Re Ga Pa).
4. Slow little alankars.
5. A short sargam geet.
6. Raag Bhoopali.

Your son enters each step through what he already loves: whole rhymes, echoing and finishing lines. Every session begins with him leading and you copying his sounds, a little softer. Only then is he offered a short phrase to echo, so the echo runs both ways.

He hears note names only when they are sung in tune by one real recorded voice, a woman or a girl (or a young boy). She sings at his own Sa, which is measured from his singing in the first week and then kept fixed for everything he knows. Much later, new pieces also come at a second Sa, slowly, so that a class or exam can work. You do not need to sing. You copy him, wait, pat, clap quietly, recite the tabla syllables (the traditional way to keep taal) and hum softly along with the recording.

The tablet is a quiet accompanist that sits beside your face:
- It never asks him to sing.
- It never shows him scores.
- It never gets louder or brighter when he gets louder.
- Most of the session it lies face-down.

He can end any session with his "all done" card, and that is always honoured. Sessions are 4 to 8 minutes. A 2-minute day counts, and missed days cost nothing. A step counts as learned only when it also happens without the screen. This is music learning and shared enjoyment, not therapy. It is built to hand over to a patient one-to-one teacher around age 5, with Prarambhik at around 7 once your local centre confirms its age rule. If the app changes aren't ready on time, the first two weeks work without the app.

## 3. The first month, week by week

**The same six steps every day.** They are on a printed picture strip on the wall and small along the top of the screen:
1. **Hello**
2. **Copycat** (he leads, you copy)
3. **Lamp** (Sa time)
4. **Song**
5. **Laya** (rhythm)
6. **Quiet ending**

**How to sit:**
- Sit on the floor facing each other.
- The tablet stands at his eye level, beside your face, so he looks at you and not at it.
- You hold the tablet and work it. He never has to touch it, except to point at one of two song cards.
- Start each session by holding "Grown-up ready?" for 2 seconds, so nothing ever plays by itself.
- **His "all done" card sits by his knee.** If he hands it to you, points to it or says "all done", the session ends then and there: a short quiet goodbye if he is calm, or simply stop if he is upset. Every time, with no make-up session.
- At the end of every session, put the card in the "done" pocket yourself and say "All done", so he learns what it means.

**After each session (about 20 seconds),** tap three answers in the grown-ups corner:
- calm / OK / wound-up
- Did we both enjoy it? (yes / a bit / no)
- Did he take turns? (none / 1-3 / 4 or more)

If something got in the way, if he used "all done", or if a particular sound came just before he got over-excited or upset, note that too.

**On a hard day,** do the **minimum day** instead: hello, one copycat turn, the quiet ending. That's 2 minutes, and it counts as a full day. There are no streaks anywhere.

### Week 0: Set-up (3 to 7 days; about 60 to 90 minutes of your time in total, plus the app work; he does nothing new)

**What you do:**
1. **Get the day-1 version of the app** (the "before day 1" group in section 9).
   - Decide now who is building it and by when. If it isn't ready, don't wait: start with the no-app version of weeks 1 and 2 (below).
   - Turn Bluetooth off.
   - Turn on Android's screen pinning for music time, so the tablet stays inside the app.
   - Run the mic check. It now shows whether the tablet has really switched off its built-in voice processing.
2. **Set the volume ceiling with him in the room.**
   - Use the loudest sound the app plays: the harmonium's Sa for now. Lower the ceiling until, at his seat, it sounds clearly softer than your normal talking voice. The tanpura is then mixed below it.
   - If you have a free sound-meter app on your phone, about 55 to 60 dB(A) at his seat is a sensible target. That figure is a design choice, not a research finding.
   - From then on, the app sets the tablet's own media volume to this level at the start of every session, and ignores the volume keys while a session runs. Until that part is built, set the tablet's media volume by hand to the same marked step before every session, because YouTube shares that volume.
   - When the singer's clips arrive in week 3, check the ceiling again with her loudest clip.
3. **Find his Sa, without asking him for anything.**
   - Whenever he starts singing a rhyme on his own, quietly tap "Listen to him" (screen dimmed) and let it run for 30 to 60 seconds. Do this on at least 3 different days.
   - The app finds the home note of his rhymes. In Twinkle and in Mary Had a Little Lamb, that is the lowest note.
   - Optionally, tap "Listen to the video" while his usual YouTube Twinkle plays nearby. Do this during his normal YouTube time, not just before or during music time, so the two stay separate. Treat the result as a hint only: the music under the singer confuses the app.
   - **Decision rule:**
     - If his home note is about the same on 3 days (within about half a key) and lands between C#4 and F4, use the nearest key as his Sa. A bonus: sargam songs that borrow his rhyme tunes will then sit in the key he already sings them in.
     - If it is very consistent every day (within about a third of a key) and also matches the video, he clearly remembers that song's key. Use it, and treat the lock as especially important. (This alone doesn't mean he has perfect pitch.)
     - Otherwise, or if "Find his Sa" isn't ready yet, use **D4 (safed 2)**. In that case, use new Bhoopali tunes instead of borrowed rhyme tunes (the borrowed-tune rule in section 4).
   - Then press **Sa lock**. From now on the harmonium, the tanpura, every recording and the future teacher all use this Sa for everything he knows.
4. **Book one singer, and line up a backup,** for a single recording session in week 2 (week 3 at the latest). She records the material for the whole first year in that one session. Who to ask and what she records are in section 5.
5. **Print:**
   - the 6-card strip with a "done" pocket
   - "Next: snack / bath / play" cards
   - his own **"all done" card**
   - a picture card for each song: three steps for "Three Steps", buns for the "Ga Re Sa song", a lamb or a kite for the third song (section 4), and a lamp for Sa
6. **Pick his best 5 minutes of the day:** not hungry, not tired, not straight after YouTube or anything loud.
7. **Ask about a hearing test.** If he hasn't had one recently, ask your paediatrician for an audiology referral: a hearing test and a check of his sound tolerance. Start the plan while you wait.

**What the app does:** listens quietly while he sings, suggests his Sa, and sets the ceiling.

**Minutes for him:** none.

### If the app isn't ready: weeks 1 and 2 without it

Nothing in weeks 1 and 2 truly needs the app. If the build slips:
- Use a shruti box set to his Sa (D4 if you haven't measured it) for Sa and the soft drone. Keep it soft.
- **Hello and goodbye:** play the hello tune slowly on a harmonium or keyboard if you have one; one finger is fine. Otherwise leave the tune out and just smile, wave and pat.
- **Lamp:** skip the face-up diya. Do kite glides with your hand or a scarf going up and down instead, each one ending while the shruti box's Sa is sounding.
- **Three Steps in week 2:** play it on the harmonium if you can; otherwise leave it until the app arrives.
- Everything else is the same: copycat, finish-the-line with word rhymes, laya, the quiet ending, the calm trials (with the shruti box alone as option 1), and the log on paper.
- The singer can tune to the shruti box at his Sa for her recording session.
- Don't use the old Level 1 test app with him in the meantime.

### Week 1 (days 1 to 7): "Awaaz" (sound play). No recordings needed. 4 to 6 minutes.

**What he does:** sits with you, makes any sounds he likes, pats along, maybe fills in a word.

**What you and the app do, step by step:**
1. **Hello (30 s).**
   - The diya's wick slowly appears. That is the "sound is coming" cue.
   - The harmonium then plays the hello tune once, on its own, the same every day.
   - You smile, wave and pat his knees to the beat. You don't need to sing.
   - The tune (at Sa = D4) is "Ga Re | Ga Re | Sa Re Ga | Ga Pa Ga Re | Sa". The words the singer will add in week 3 are "Hel-lo, hel-lo, [his name] / time for mu-sic now".
2. **Copycat (1 to 2 min, screen face-down; a soft tanpura may play underneath).**
   - Whatever sound he makes (a hum, a squeal, a bit of a rhyme), copy it straight back: a little softer, about the same length and shape.
   - Then wait 3 to 5 seconds with an interested face and an open palm. No words.
   - If he is quiet for about 20 seconds, do one soft rising-and-falling "whoooo" with your hand going up and down, then wait again.
3. **Lamp: free sing (about 1 minute; screen up; no tanpura).**
   - The diya follows his voice. A higher voice makes the diya go higher.
   - There is no target and no reward. Watch him, not the screen.
   - This is the only face-up moment this week.
4. **Song (1 to 2 min, screen face-down).**
   - Say or chant his favourite rhyme in its usual English words, in a rhythm, patting both knees once per syllable. No sargam.
   - From day 2, stop before the last word of a line ("Twinkle twinkle little ..."). Hold out an open palm and wait up to 5 seconds.
   - Any sound counts. If nothing comes, say the word warmly and carry on.
5. **Laya (30 s).** Pats on knees, then finger-taps on your palm, then a silent wave, then hands on lap: "big, small, tiny, still".
6. **Quiet ending (1 min).**
   - A soft tanpura fades in. Three small diyas on the screen go out one by one: a visible countdown.
   - The harmonium plays the hello tune once more, slowly. This is the goodbye.
   - The tanpura fades over 5 seconds. Put his card in the done pocket, say "All done", then show and name the next card: "Next: snack."

**If a sound seems to upset him** (he covers his ears, flinches, cries or moves away), don't copy or wait: fade the sound out at once and end that step (section 6, "When a sound upsets him").

**Calm trials:** on three days this week (days 3, 5 and 6, or whenever he gets wound up), try one calm option each in the quiet ending:
- (1) the tanpura alone
- (2) you humming one long soft "mmm" with the tanpura
- (3) six slow pats on his back or knees

Note which settled him best.

**Day 4 is an app-off day:** the same routine face to face, with no tablet. **Day 7:** rest or a minimum day.

**Through the day:** copy his sounds during play a few times; play finish-the-line with word rhymes in the bath or car.

### Week 2 (days 8 to 14): "Sa is home". 5 to 6 minutes.

The same six steps, with these additions:
- **Lamp (60 to 90 s):**
  - **First a "sing together" moment.** The harmonium plays Sa with the tanpura and you hum softly along for 20 to 30 seconds. The diya glows steadily and does not listen or judge.
  - **Then two "listen and wait" turns.** The harmonium plays Sa, the sound fades, the diya's ring opens, you show an open palm, and everyone waits 5 seconds. If he sings, the diya simply follows him. Nothing is required and nothing is scored.
- **Kite glides** (inside free sing): he slides his voice up and down on "whoo" and the diya rises and falls. At the end of each glide the harmonium plays one soft Sa, so every glide "comes home".
- **Song:** his word rhyme as before, plus a "listen and move" game. The harmonium plays the "Three Steps" melody: Sa Re Ga, Ga Re Sa (D4, E4, F#4, E4, D4).
  - Your hands show the heights: Sa on the tummy, Re on the chest, Ga at the chin.
  - Both of you pat on every note.
- **The singer's recording session happens this week** (or next). She records everything for the first year in one go (section 5).
- **New this week, the one new sound: a listening corner.** At wind-down, play 5 to 10 minutes of a real, soft, sung Bhoopali aalap at low volume. Audio only, from your usual music app.
- **"Hear Sa" before bath and meals:** tap it (the screen can stay off), let the Sa play, and hum along with it. Never hum Sa without it, so your Sa never drifts.
- **Your own practice (optional):** 2 minutes a day out of his earshot, with earphones. The app's private tuner helps you hum along with Sa.
- **Day 11:** app-off day.
- **Day 14 review:**
  - Pin his best calm option as his Calm setting.
  - Any step that upset him: drop it for a week and bring it back later.

### Week 3 (days 15 to 21): "Didi sings" (the singer's voice is this week's one new sound). 6 to 7 minutes.

**The day before:** show him the singer's photo card.

- **Hello and goodbye:** now the singer's recorded voice sings the words over the *same* harmonium tune, at the same pitch and speed. Nothing else changes.
- **Lamp:**
  - First sing together with the singer's 3-second "Saaa" (or "mmm"). The diya glows steadily and does not judge.
  - Then one or two "listen and wait" turns, with her "Saaa" as the model. The diya follows him. Nothing is scored, though the app quietly logs.
- **Song: "Three Steps" in sargam,** sung by her.
  - On the first two days he only listens and pats: the whole song twice.
  - From day 3, the finish-the-line version: she sings "Sa Re Ga, Ga Re ..." and stops. The ring opens, there is your open palm, and 5 seconds of silence.
  - **Any sound makes the song carry on. That carrying on is the reward.**
  - If he is silent, she sings "Sa" and the song carries on, with no comment.
  - **You stay silent during his gap:** no humming, singing or saying the note. Any voice keeps the song going, and the app can't tell your voice from his, so your silence keeps the log honest.
  - The first gap is always the final Sa. In raag music every phrase comes home to Sa (this is called *nyas*), so the first note he owns is the home note.
- **First-then:** the strip shows "First Three Steps, then your rhyme". His English rhyme comes straight after, as his choice.
- **Day 18:** app-off day.

### Week 4 (days 22 to 28): "Laya enters". 6 to 8 minutes.

- **Copycat:** 3 or 4 turns where he leads. Then one 2-note phrase from the singer ("Sa Sa" or "Sa Re Sa"), the ring opens, and you wait 5 seconds in silence. Any reply counts. The app notes the shape privately.
- **Lamp, with the first gently scored turn:**
  - After the sing-together, the singer sings "Saaa" and the ring opens.
  - If he holds an unbroken tone for 0.6 seconds that sits near middle Sa (its middle pitch within ±75 cents) and doesn't wobble too much (the app allows a wide wobble at first), the diya glows slowly and the music moves on to his song. That is the whole reward.
  - At most 3 turns. Stop at the first success.
- **Song:** a 2-card choice between "Three Steps" and the new **"Ga Re Sa song"**: "Ga Re Sa | Ga Re Sa | Sa Sa Sa Sa Re Re Re Re | Ga Re Sa". It borrows the Hot Cross Buns tune; if he already knows that rhyme, the borrowed-tune rule in section 4 applies. Listen for two days, then leave the final Sa as the gap.
- **Laya: Keherwa, spoken by you (padhant).**
  - Slowly, about one syllable a second: "Dha Ge Na Ti | Na Ka Dhi Na".
  - **One quiet clap on beat 1 (sam):** two fingers on your palm, or a pat on your thigh.
  - Soft pats on beats 2 to 4.
  - **An open-hand wave on beat 5 (khali).**
  - Soft pats on beats 6 to 8.
  - Do 2 to 4 cycles, then come down: pats, finger-taps, silent wave, hands on lap.
  - No tabla sound yet.
- **Two app-off days this week, and every week from now on.**
- **Day 28 review (10 minutes, grown-ups corner):**
  - Record 30 seconds of him singing a favourite rhyme and rate his singing voice: chants / speaking voice / light singing voice / wider range.
  - Mark each song calm / OK / wound-up.
  - Read the "sang it on his own" entries, the "what got in the way" notes, and any "all done" or upset-by-a-sound notes.
  - Then carry on into Stage 1 (section 4).

**Add nothing else in month 1.**

| Week | Minutes a day | New for him | Screen face-up |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0 | nothing | 0 |
| 1 | 4-6 | harmonium hello, free-sing diya | about 1 min |
| 2 | 5-6 | harmonium Sa, kite glides, listening corner | about 2 min |
| 3 | 6-7 | the singer's voice, Three Steps in sargam | about 3 min |
| 4 | 6-8 | Ga Re Sa song, Keherwa bols with quiet claps, first gently scored Sa turn | about 3-4 min |

## 4. The levels after that

**One stage at a time, with three threads always running alongside.** The grown-ups corner always shows "Current stage: ___" in one line, so you know where you are.

**The three threads:**
- **Sa thread** (the Lamp step, every day). It has its own milestones and **never holds back the songs or the echoes**:
  - **S1:** he joins in the sing-together with a light singing voice (no pitch judged).
  - **S2** (from week 4): after the singer's "Saaa", he holds an unbroken tone for 0.6 seconds, with its middle pitch within ±75 cents of middle Sa and its wobble inside the starting limit (wide, about 50 to 60 cents).
  - **S3** (around months 3 to 8): **after the tanpura alone** (it sounds Sa, then fades before his turn), with no voice model, he finds Sa within about ±60 cents and holds it about 1 second. The diya is hidden, but the app is still listening. This is the first real *sur* milestone.
  - **S4** (around months 6 to 12): Sa within ±40 to 50 cents, held 1 to 1.5 seconds, with the diya hidden.
  - **S5** (before a teacher takes over): within ±25 to 30 cents, held up to 2 to 3 seconds. Never "as long as you can".
  - **How the limits change:** the Sa tolerance tightens by 10 cents after 4 good turns out of 5 on two separate days, and loosens by 10 after a hard day. The wobble limit tightens more slowly. You approve each change.
- **Laya thread.** Each new taal or tabla sound counts as that week's one new sound, and enters at 30 to 50 percent of the volume ceiling:
  1. Pats on every sung note (month 1).
  2. Keherwa spoken, with a quiet clap on sam and a wave on khali (from week 4).
  3. Dadra (Stage 2): "Dha Dhi Na | Dha Ti Na", quiet clap on beat 1, wave on beat 4. Dadra is a separate 6-beat taal, not a "fast Keherwa".
  4. A very soft recorded tabla theka (Stage 2). Prarambhik is sung with tabla, so he needs to get used to it.
  5. Audible claps (Stage 2 or 3): real hand claps, light and slow, away from his face. Prarambhik asks him to show taal with claps and waves, so he needs these eventually, but only once the log shows he is comfortable.
  6. Stamping the sam, on a cushion or mat only (Stage 2), and kept only if the log stays calm.
  7. Teentaal (Stage 4): clap on 1, 5 and 13; wave on 9.
- **Listening thread:**
  - the bedtime listening corner of real sung Bhoopali (later Yaman)
  - "Hear Sa" before bath and meals
  - letting him sit beside any adult who does riyaz

**The borrowed-tune rule.** Some sargam songs borrow the tune of an English rhyme: Mary (Stage 1), Rain Rain (Stage 2), Twinkle as "the Bilawal song" (Stage 3), and Hot Cross Buns for the Ga Re Sa song. Use a borrowed tune he already knows only if his Sa was set from his own home note in week 0. Then the sargam version sits in the key he already sings it in. If his Sa is the D4 default, use the new Bhoopali tune listed instead, so that a tune he knows never turns up in a key he doesn't:
- instead of the Ga Re Sa song or Mary in sargam: **the Kite song** (Sa Re Ga Ga | Pa Pa Ga – | Ga Re Ga Re | Sa – – –), with a kite card. If both are ruled out, the third song is lines 1 and 2 of the sargam geet (Stage 3), sung as a short song of their own.
- instead of Rain Rain: a new Dha phrase, "Ga Pa Dha Pa | Ga Re Sa –"
- instead of the Bilawal song: leave it out. The Bilawal alankars bring in Ma.

Never change the tunes or keys of his English rhymes themselves.

### Stage 1: "Sa is home" (about months 1 to 3)

**Goal:** he enjoys the daily routine, takes back-and-forth sound turns with you, and sings the missing note in familiar sargam songs. He learns that Sa is where songs come home.

**Activities:**
- Copycat first, every day.
- Sing-together on Sa, then up to 3 listened Sa turns.
- Kite glides that end on Sa.
- Three songs, each a new song with its own picture card:
  - **Three Steps:** Sa Re Ga, Ga Re Sa.
  - **Ga Re Sa song:** Ga Re Sa | Ga Re Sa | Sa Sa Sa Sa Re Re Re Re | Ga Re Sa (or the Kite song, by the borrowed-tune rule).
  - **The third song** (added in month 2), by the borrowed-tune rule: either **Mary in sargam** (Ga Re Sa Re Ga Ga Ga | Re Re Re | Ga Pa Pa | Ga Re Sa Re Ga Ga Ga Ga | Re Re Ga Re Sa) or **the Kite song**.
  - All of them use only Bhoopali notes, stay between Sa and Pa, and end on Sa.
- How the gaps grow: the final Sa first, then other line endings (a Ga or a Pa at the end of a line), then two gaps in one song.
- 2-note echo phrases after copycat: Sa-Sa, Sa-Re-Sa, Re-Sa.
- One sing-together turn per session.
- Keherwa spoken, with quiet claps.

**Move on when:**
- On 3 separate calm days, he fills the final gap with a **sung** (not spoken) sound in most tries, in at least two songs.
- At least once with the tablet face-down (the app listens and logs; you can hear "sung, not spoken" yourself).
- Count only gaps where you stayed silent.
- Pitch accuracy is *not* needed. How close he was only goes in the log.

**App's role:**
- plays the singer's clips line by line
- stops, opens the ring and waits 5 seconds
- any voice makes the song carry on
- a voice whose middle pitch is within about ±100 cents of the target note, unbroken for 0.4 seconds and not wobbling too much, also gives a slow warm glow
- silence means the singer sings the note and carries on
- stores each pitch trace for you
- the diya glows on every success

### Stage 2: "Echo me, echo you" (about months 3 to 6)

**Goal:**
- **Echoes:** he echoes 2- to 4-note phrases with the right shape (up, down, same).
- **Bell song:** he sings Sa-Pa-Sa in the middle octave (D4, A4, D4), with your hands going from tummy to the top of your head and back.
- **More gaps:** he finishes lines on Re, Ga and Pa, not only Sa.
- **His own tunes:** he makes up tiny tunes of his own.
- **Rhythm:** he claps sam and waves khali with you, and stays calm over a very soft tabla theka.

**Activities:**
- **Question-and-answer rounds:** 3 copycat turns where he leads, then 3 phrases from the singer:
  - Sa-Re, Re-Sa, Sa-Re-Ga, Ga-Re-Sa
  - Ga-Pa, Pa-Ga, Ga-Pa-Ga
  - Sa-Pa-Sa, Pa-Ga-Re-Sa
  - These are all fragments of Bhoopali.
- **How each phrase moves on, over the weeks:**
  1. You sing it together.
  2. Model first, then he echoes.
  3. A softer model.
  4. Only the first note.
  5. On his own.
- **Hand signs** (height shown by the body, not by words): Sa on the tummy, Re on the chest, Ga at the chin, Pa on top of the head. Later, Ma at the nose and Dha above the head.
- **"Your tune":** he makes up 2 or 3 notes and you, or the app, copy it back.
- **App copycat** (introduced as a new-sound week): it plays his own phrase back softly at his own pitch. Keep it only if he stays calm.
- **Rain Rain in sargam** (Pa Ga Pa Pa Ga, Pa Pa Ga Dha Pa Pa Ga), or the new Dha phrase by the borrowed-tune rule, only if Dha (B4) is comfortable for him.
- Dadra.
- The soft theka, one short cycle at a time.
- Audible claps, as one week's new sound, if the log has been calm.
- **Top-up recording (only if needed):** everything for Stages 2 and 3 was recorded in session 1. A top-up with the same singer at the same Sa is only for anything new.

**Move on when:**
- On 3 separate days, he gets the right shape on most 2- and 3-note phrases. The app judges this, even with the tablet face-down.
- At least one of those days is screen-down.
- He sings two sargam songs whole with the recording, calmly.
- He stays calm over the soft theka.

**App's role:**
- It judges the whole reply *after* the phrase ends, never note by note while he sings.
- It compares the shape to the model, measured from his own first note, so the key he sings in doesn't matter.
- You can replay his line beside the model's line.
- The glow now comes on about 2 successes in 3.

### Stage 3: "Sargam geet and slow alankars" (about months 6 to 12, age about 4½ to 5)

**Goal:** he sings a 4-line sargam geet in Bhoopali notes and his first slow alankars, as little songs. He also sings once for a familiar adult who isn't you, and starts to meet new phrases at a second Sa.

**Activities:**
- **The sargam geet.** Each line is one Keherwa cycle, with sam on the first note of the line. Learn it by echo, one line a week:
  - Line 1: Sa Re Ga – | Ga Re Sa –
  - Line 2: Ga Pa Ga – | Ga Re Sa –
  - Line 3: Sa Re Ga Pa | Ga – Re –
  - Line 4: Pa Ga Re Ga | Re – Sa –
  - When his range reaches Dha, add the line "Ga Pa Dha Pa | Ga Re Sa –". From then on it really sounds like Raag Bhoopali.
- **Slow alankars,** one note per beat at about one beat a second:
  - Bhoopali groups: Sa Re Ga, Re Ga Pa, then back down Pa Ga Re, Ga Re Sa.
  - Then Bilawal groups, which introduce Ma: Sa Re Ga, Re Ga Ma, Ga Ma Pa.
- **"The Bilawal song"** (Twinkle in sargam: Sa Sa Pa Pa Dha Dha Pa | Ma Ma Ga Ga Re Re Sa), only if the borrowed-tune rule allows it. This is a new song with a star card, used only once Dha is comfortable. Never present it as a correction of the English Twinkle.
- **"Which song is this?":** the singer sings the first 3 notes of a song he knows and he carries it on. This is the seed of raag recognition.
- **A second Sa, for new material only.** Once he is settled in this stage, show a picture story first. Then let him hear a few *new* short phrases at a second Sa, one key away: phrases he has never heard at his home Sa (they were recorded in session 1). Start with listening, then echoing. His known songs always stay at his home Sa. If it upsets him, drop it for a month and try again. This is what later lets him join a class, where everyone sings at one Sa.
- Keherwa stepping on 8 floor mats, with a bigger one for sam.
- **A new listener:** a grandparent sits in on one session.

**Move on when:**
- He sings the whole sargam geet with the recording on 3 calm days.
- He sings two alankars slowly with the right shape.
- His app-off days look like his app days.
- He has sung once with a new familiar adult present.

**App's role:**
- the diya glows only at the end of a step
- target tolerance about ±40 to 50 cents
- a soft Dadra theka
- the harmonium and tanpura at the second Sa, for the new phrases only
- an export of clips and pitch traces for a possible teacher

### Stage 4: "Raag Bhoopali and a teacher trial" (about age 5 to 6)

**Goal:**
- He sings Bhoopali going up and coming down, as far as his range allows (Sa Re Ga Pa Dha Sa'). Upper Sa comes only once he reaches it on his own; low notes are still for listening.
- He sings simple Bhoopali phrases: Ga Re Sa; Pa Ga; Dha Pa Ga Re Sa.
- He sings the sargam geet, or a simple bandish from the teacher, over a soft Teentaal theka.
- He sings alankars slowly, then at medium speed (two notes per beat).
- He tells Bhoopali tunes from Bilawal tunes. Yaman is for listening only at this stage.
- He keeps Keherwa, Dadra and Teentaal with clap and wave.
- He sings calmly for a new adult.
- He sings new material calmly at a second Sa as well as his own.

**Activities:**
- **A one-to-one teacher, as an 8-week trial,** with quiet 15- to 20-minute lessons and you present.
- **Agree the Sa with the teacher** before lessons start: the pieces he knows stay at his Sa; new pieces may come at the teacher's Sa, building on the second-Sa step.
- The **"new adult" ladder:** grandparent, then neighbour, then a video call with the teacher, then a visit at a quiet time, then trial lessons.
- Give the teacher the hand-over pack (section 5) and the "How he learns" note (section 8).

**Move on when:**
- The teacher judges he can learn new material directly from them.
- He is calm in the teacher's space.
- If the first teacher doesn't fit, try another. Choose for patience and willingness to adapt more than reputation.

**App's role:**
- it becomes the home practice partner: the teacher's clips at the agreed Sa, the tanpura and the theka
- the diya is mostly off, with traces kept for you
- the log is shared with the teacher if you choose

### Stage 5: "Ready for class" (about age 6 to 7)

**Goal:** he is ready for the Prarambhik exam at around 7, if your local centre's current rules allow it. The exam is run through a local centre by ABGMVM or another board, so ask the centre for its age rule and syllabus early. That means:
- aaroh-avaroh and recognition of the syllabus raags (Bhoopali, Yaman, and Bilawal where the board asks for it)
- alankars at slow and medium speed
- reciting Teentaal, Dadra and Keherwa with hand gestures
- singing with tabla
- singing at the Sa that will be used on the day, agreed in advance with the centre or the accompanist
- coping with a new room, a new adult and a class's shared Sa

**Activities:**
- a Balgeet-style small class or continued one-to-one lessons; avoid big group practice rooms at first
- practising at the agreed exam Sa for a few months beforehand
- a mock audition at home with an unfamiliar adult
- short visits to the exam centre at quiet times
- a picture story of exam day

**Move on when:** the teacher recommends him for entry, and he sings calmly for an unfamiliar adult in an unfamiliar room.

**App's role:** a tanpura and theka practice partner, plus the log. No diya on exam pieces.

### A realistic 12-month goal

By about age 5:
- a light singing voice between Sa and Pa
- Sa within about one harmonium key on most days
- 2- to 4-note echoes with the right shape
- 3 to 5 sargam songs
- pats and claps roughly in time with you

Full alankars and the whole raag are 2- to 3-year goals. If he hears more than he can yet sing, that is normal. Slow singing does not mean slow learning.

## 5. Where the voice comes from, now and later

**Who does what:**

| Sound | Its job | Never |
|---|---|---|
| **One recorded singer** (a woman or a girl; if a boy, one under about 11) | every sung note name; the Sa model; songs; echo phrases; the hello and goodbye words | re-recorded casually |
| **Harmonium and tanpura** (generated inside the app, always exactly in tune) | the base layer from day 1: the hello tune, the starting note, the drone, a reference for you | the only model for note names |
| **You** | copying his sounds, waiting, pats and quiet claps, spoken tabla bols, humming softly *along with* the recording | speaking note names; singing alone as his model; making any sound during his turn |
| **His own voice** | played back to him in copycat (Stage 2, optional) | played while the mic is listening |
| **A synthetic or text-to-speech voice** | none | ever |

### Now (book in week 0, record in week 2 or 3)

**Who to ask (any one person with a steady, plain voice):**
- a female relative or neighbour
- an older girl who sings, or a boy under about 11
- a senior Gandharva or music-college student
- a school or college music teacher
- a temple or bhajan singer
- an online classical teacher, who records at home and sends the files back

A woman or a girl is the safest choice. If you choose a boy, pick one under about 11: a boy's voice can break within months, and later recordings need to match the first ones.

**Line up a backup singer too:** someone who could record new material if your first singer moves away or becomes unavailable. You won't use her unless you need to. If you do, her voice is that week's one new sound, previewed with a picture card, and the songs he already knows keep the first singer's takes.

A small fee is normal. She signs a one-page consent for family use. Take one photo of her for the "Didi sings" card. Use a still photo, not a video: a singing face on the tablet could switch on his YouTube habit and pull his eyes away from yours.

**How she records (one session of about 60 to 90 minutes with breaks, or two sittings in the same week):**
- She records everything for roughly the first year in this one go, so nothing depends on finding her again months later.
- **At HIS Sa,** not her own. His Sa (usually D4) is higher than many women's usual Sa.
- She listens to the app's harmonium and tanpura (or a shruti box at his Sa) in one earphone while she sings. The hello and goodbye are sung over the app's own hello track, so they line up exactly with what he heard in weeks 1 and 2.
- **Soft, plain, straight notes:** no meend (slides), gamak (shakes), vibrato or concert-style emotion.
- A steady slow tempo of about one note a second.

**If no singer can come in person:** the app exports the backing tracks at his Sa, plus the list of clips. Send them to an online teacher by WhatsApp. She sings along with earphones on her phone and sends the files back.

**What the app checks for every clip:**
- It measures the pitch. Anything more than 30 cents (about a third of a key) off his Sa is sent back for a retake.
- If a whole take is slightly sharp or flat all the way through, the app may shift the whole take by one small amount (at most about 20 to 30 cents). It never corrects notes one by one. Listen to it afterwards; if it sounds at all odd, ask for a retake instead.
- It trims silences and matches the volume to the ceiling.
- It cuts the "gap" versions from the same full take, so the gap song sounds exactly like the song he knows. At first this can be done by hand on a computer (for example in Audacity, a free audio editor).
- Each take is then **kept as the one he hears,** because the first version he learns tends to be the one he keeps.
- **Master copies are kept off the tablet.** Save the raw recordings and the final clips (and later his Sa setting and the log) to a private place you control, such as an encrypted family drive or a family PC. Then a broken tablet, cleared app data or a reinstall never forces a swap.

**The clip list (about 60 clips):**
- **For the first months:**
  - "Saaa" three times (3 s each), and "mmm" on Sa twice.
  - Re, Ga and Pa, held for 2 s each.
  - Each of these phrases, once in sargam and once on "aa":
    - Sa-Sa, Sa-Re, Re-Sa, Sa-Re-Sa, Sa-Re-Ga, Ga-Re-Sa
    - Ga-Pa, Pa-Ga, Ga-Pa-Ga, Sa-Pa-Sa (middle octave), Pa-Ga-Re-Sa
  - The hello and goodbye, with his name.
  - Whole songs: "Three Steps", the "Ga Re Sa song" and the third song, each in the version the borrowed-tune rule picks (section 4).
  - Two short sung lines that are fine to hear echoed all day: "Again" (on Sa) and "All done" (Ga Re Sa).
- **For later (kept until needed):**
  - the sargam geet lines, plus the extra Dha line
  - the Bhoopali and Bilawal alankars
  - Rain Rain in sargam, or the new Dha phrase (borrowed-tune rule)
  - "The Bilawal song", only if the borrowed-tune rule allows it
  - three or four new short phrases at a second Sa one key away, for the second-Sa step in Stage 3

**Later top-ups (only if needed; same singer, same Sa):** for anything new, such as songs a teacher asks for.

**If no singer is found by week 3:**
- Carry on with the harmonium. Nothing is lost, because the harmonium stays as the base layer and the singer is simply added on top later.
- Nobody speaks note names in the meantime.
- Keep asking; the online teacher option works from anywhere.
- We no longer recommend recording your own "Saaa" as his model. It would have to be swapped later, and swaps are exactly what an echolalic child who likes sameness resists.

### Your own voice

You are a fellow student, not the performer. The plan never depends on you singing in tune.

**Your musical jobs:**
- copying his sounds (any pitch is fine, because you are copying him, not modelling a target)
- the open palm and the silent wait
- pats and quiet claps
- **reciting the tabla bols:** speaking them in rhythm is the traditional way and needs no singing
- humming softly *with* the recording or with "Hear Sa"

Your own sounds follow the same sound rules as the app: quiet claps, a soft voice, nothing sudden (section 6).

**Optional private practice,** if you'd like to hum more confidently:
- 2 minutes a day, with earphones, out of his earshot
- hum with the recorded Sa while the app's private tuner shows how close you are
- then Sa-Pa-Sa, then Ga Re Sa

This follows how an Indian music therapist, Rajam Shanker, trains mothers who are not singers. Over the months you may sing a little more along with the recording in the sing-together turns, but never alone as his model.

**If the parent at home is his father:** a man's voice sits an octave lower and can pull a child down into his talking voice. Leave the pitch to the recording, or hum lightly in a high, light falsetto. Take charge of the rhythm.

### Later (a teacher, around age 5)

- **What to give the teacher:**
  - his Sa as a note, a frequency and a harmonium key (for example, D4 = 293.66 Hz = safed 2)
  - how far he has got with the second Sa
  - the clip list and the recordings
  - the songs he knows
  - his pitch traces
  - a summary of the log
  - the "How he learns" note
- **What to ask the teacher for:** plain, soft aakar and sargam, sung at the agreed Sa and his tempo.
- **New material:** comes in the teacher's voice, at his Sa or at the teacher's Sa, as agreed.
- **Songs he already knows:** keep the singer's takes, at his Sa. If the teacher wants to replace one, alternate the old and new versions for 2 to 4 weeks, with a picture story, before retiring the old one.

## 6. Sound, calm and over-excitement: exact rules

**Sound**
1. **One volume ceiling, kept by the tablet itself.**
   - Set it in week 0 with him present, using the loudest sound the app plays (the harmonium's Sa now; the singer's loudest clip once it arrives). It should be clearly softer than your normal talking voice at his seat (about 55 to 60 dB(A) if you use a meter).
   - The app's own volume slider only limits sound inside the app. The tablet's media volume sits on top of it and is shared with YouTube. So at the start of every session the app sets the tablet's media volume to the calibrated level, and while a session runs it ignores the volume keys or puts the level straight back.
   - Until that is built, set the media volume by hand to the marked step before every session.
2. **The tanpura sits 6 to 10 dB below the singer's voice,** so the voice is always clear without anything being loud. Because the ceiling is set with the loudest sound, everything else sits below it.
3. **Nothing starts suddenly.**
   - A 1-second visual cue comes first: the diya's wick appears.
   - Voices and notes fade in over at least 0.3 seconds, the tanpura over 2 to 3 seconds.
   - Sounds fade out; they never cut off.
4. **Your own sounds count too.**
   - A clap close to his face is much louder and sharper than anything the app plays, and it starts suddenly.
   - So the default taali is a quiet clap: two fingers on your palm, or a pat on your thigh.
   - Real, audible claps come in only as one week's new sound (Stage 2 or later), light and slow, away from his face, and stay only if the log stays calm.
   - Keep your voice soft too. No sudden "Yay!" or loud praise.
5. **No beeps, chimes, cheers, applause, "wrong" sounds or high-pitched effects.**
   - The reward is never a sound effect, and never "louder". When he succeeds, the music simply continues.
   - (An earlier idea, making the tanpura swell when he succeeds, was dropped because it adds loudness.)
6. **At most one new sound per week.** That includes the singer, a backup singer, his own voice played back, the tabla theka, a new harmonium register, a second Sa, a new raag recording, audible claps and stamping.
   - It enters at 30 to 50 percent of the ceiling, for one short go.
   - It gets louder or longer only if the log stays calm.
   - If something winds him up or upsets him twice, drop it for a week.
7. **Offer "mmm" as well as "aa".** Humming is traditional, quieter and less exciting, and the app still hears its pitch.
8. **The ending is always the quietest part.** Sessions are 8 minutes or less, never more than 10. End before he tires.
9. **Do not make noise-cancelling headphones the fix.** They may increase sensitivity over time, and they break the shared play and the microphone set-up. Keep the speaker low instead.

**Visuals**

10. **The screen:**
    - a dark, warm, muted background
    - one small diya at the edge near your face
    - the glow eases up and down over about half a second and never flashes
    - no confetti, stars or Diwali-style fireworks
    - a setting lets you choose steady glow, gentle glow, or glow with soft rays
11. **Predictable layout:** the same screen every day, the strip always at the top and your icon always in the same corner. Nothing changes on its own. No pop-ups.
12. **Loudness never changes what he sees.** The diya responds only to pitch and steadiness. If he gets very loud, it holds still or softens slightly, so shouting earns nothing new.

**The "big moment"**

13. His over-excitement may partly be him seeking a big sensation, so he gets one sanctioned big movement, always followed by a fixed way down.
    - The default is **soft two-handed pats on the sam.**
    - Stamping on the sam is added only in Stage 2, only on a cushion or mat, as that week's new sound, and kept only if the log stays calm for two weeks.
    - Always come down the ladder afterwards: pats, then finger-taps on the palm, then a silent wave (the khali), then hands on lap, then the quiet ending.

**His way to stop**

14. **His "all done" card** (or a word or gesture he already uses for "finished") ends the session at any time.
    - It is always honoured, straight away: a short quiet goodbye if he is calm, or simply stop if he is upset.
    - No coaxing to finish, and no make-up session.
    - Note in the log when he uses it and what came just before. Using it often is useful information, not a failure.

**When a sound upsets him**

15. **Distress is different from over-excitement.** Signs include covering his ears, flinching, crying, going very still, moving away or leaving.
    - Fade the current sound out at once (hold Calm; it fades within about a second, never a hard cut).
    - End that step. Don't copy him and don't wait for him.
    - Offer quiet, his calm option, or the end of the session if he wants it.
    - Log the sound that came just before.
    - Leave that sound out for at least a week. Bring it back only at a lower level, as that week's one new sound.

**Calm mode**

16. **A small Calm icon for you on every screen** (hold it for 1 second). It:
    - fades all sound to his pinned calm option
    - dims the screen
    - sets the diya to a steady glow that ignores the microphone
    - shows you a short prompt: "Hum one long soft mmm with him, or just sit together."
17. **His calm option is chosen from the week-1 trials,** because children differ so much: tanpura alone, you humming Sa, or slow pats. Pin the winner by day 14.

**When he gets wound up**

18. **"Copy him, but smaller."** This is for excitement. If he looks upset by a sound, use rule 15 instead.
    - Copy his loud sound once, more softly. Then go a little softer and slower each time.
    - Do not add big claps or louder pats.
    - Nothing new happens on the screen.
    - If he hasn't settled in about a minute, tap Calm.
    - If that doesn't work, play the goodbye and stop. There is no make-up session.
    - Matching his energy and then slowing down is common clinical practice but has not been properly tested. Watch what actually works for him.
19. **Afterwards, log the sound that came just before.** Review the volume and sounds once a month, because his tolerance can change.
20. **Get two checks if you can:** a hearing test and sound-tolerance check by an audiologist (your paediatrician can refer you), and an occupational therapist's sensory profile. For a programme built on pitch and sound, the hearing test comes first. Both help before fine-tuning volume and glow intensity.

**Later classes**

21. Start one-to-one or in a very small group, in a quiet room. Make the first visits at a quiet time and keep early lessons short. Avoid rooms where many students practise at once.

## 7. What the microphone feedback should and should not do

*This section is mainly for whoever builds the app. Line numbers refer to the current test app, [index.html](../index.html). Section 9 says which of these is needed by when.*

### It should

1. **Count only a held note, and judge it with two separate checks.**
   - Today the app adds up every in-tune moment across a 9-second turn (`held+=dt`, line 424). Instead, require one unbroken hold: it resets if he stops, or slides outside the tolerance, for more than 150 ms.
   - **Accuracy check:** the middle (median) pitch of the hold is within the current tolerance of middle Sa. This starts at ±75 cents (point 2).
   - **Steadiness check:** the spread of pitch within the hold stays under a wobble limit. This starts wide, at about 50 to 60 cents, because young voices wobble, and tightens slowly.
   - Start at 0.6 seconds. Add 0.1 seconds only after 4 successes in 5 on two separate days.
   - Cap the hold at 1.5 s in Stages 1 and 2, and at 2 to 3 s later.
2. **Start wide and adapt.**
   - Replace the fixed TOL/HOLD presets (45/30/18 cents and 0.9/1.2/1.5 s, line 194) with a starting point of ±75 cents and 0.6 s.
   - Tighten or loosen by 10 cents as in section 4, with your approval each time. The floor is 25 to 30 cents before a teacher takes over.
   - Add an "any steady voice" setting with no pitch judgement at all.
3. **Make higher always look higher.**
   - Show the diya's height as the true distance from his middle Sa, kept within about −300 to +950 cents. At the moment pitch is folded into one octave (`centsToSa`, line 316), so Pa and Dha show *below* Sa.
   - If the reading suddenly jumps by an octave (about 1200 cents) between frames, use the octave nearest the previous frame.
4. **Count middle Sa only for success.**
   - While upper Sa (D5) is still listening-only, a strained upper Sa earns no credit. It is just noted in the log.
   - Low Sa falls outside his listening band anyway.
5. **Listen only in a child's range during his turns and free sing: 180 to 1000 Hz.** Today it accepts 70 to 1100 Hz (line 310). Keep the wide range only for the mic check and your private tuner. This stops a father's voice, fan hum and male TV voices from moving the diya. It cannot filter out your voice, which sits in the same range, so you stay silent during his listening windows. Otherwise the log can't be trusted.
6. **Use the same turn pattern every time:**
   - the model sings
   - the sound fades
   - 800 ms of silence
   - the turn cue: the diya's ring opens and you show your open palm
   - an optional very short, soft pluck on **Pa, never Sa**, which has died away before listening starts. It is off by default; if he starts echoing the pluck note, leave it off.
   - listening begins 250 ms after the turn opens and lasts up to 5 seconds
   - **Success:** a slow glow and the music moves on.
   - **Silence:** the model sings once more, the next try is sung together, then the music moves on.
   - At most 3 Sa turns. Stop at the first success.
7. **Offer clear listening modes,** chosen by you on each step card:
   - **Free sing:** the diya follows him, with no target.
   - **Sa call:** call and response, with the model silent while he sings.
   - **Sing together:** the model and tanpura play. The diya **does not listen at all;** it simply glows steadily. While the app's own speaker is singing, the mic cannot tell his voice from hers, so it should not pretend to judge.
   - **Finish the line:** a 5-second window after the line stops.
     - Any voice keeps the song going (which is why you stay silent).
     - If the middle pitch is within about ±100 cents of the target note, unbroken for 0.4 s and passing the steadiness check, it also gives a slow glow.
     - Silence: the singer fills the note.
   - **Echo (Stage 2):** record the whole reply, split it into notes (each steady for at least 120 ms within ±40 cents), then judge it after it ends.
     - First check the shape: up, down or same, measured from his own first note.
     - Later check the steps, within ±100 cents.
     - Ignore timing.
     - You see his trace beside the model's; he sees only a gentle glow.
   - **Copycat playback (optional, Stage 2):** record 1 to 3 seconds of his voice and play it back softly at his own pitch, with short fades. The mic is muted during playback.
8. **Ignore loudness.** The glow and height come from pitch and steadiness only. Above about −12 dBFS at the input, the glow holds still or softens slightly.
9. **Keep his scored turns drone-free.**
   - Keep the tanpura fade-out during scored turns (`tpDuck`, lines 263-267), and keep free sing silent, with no drone.
   - Switch off and hide the current "Tanpura while he sings" setting (line 151). At line 266 it keeps the full drone at 45 percent, which the app can mistake for his Sa.
   - The earlier idea of a Pa-only drone is dropped. The tanpura's whole job is to sound Sa, and a drone with no Sa in it might teach him that Pa is home.
   - If a drone during his turns is ever wanted, keep the full drone and have the app subtract its own drone from the microphone signal. It knows exactly what it is playing, so an adaptive filter in the background audio thread can use that as its reference. This is untested on the tablet.
   - Also add a filter that ignores pitches within about 3 cents of a tanpura string that vary by less than 2 cents over 300 ms. Real voices waver; the app's strings don't. Apply this to free sing and copycat too, whenever any drone is playing.
   - Ship any drone-during-his-turn mode only when the mic check passes **"drone alone keeps the diya dark for 10 seconds"** on your Lenovo, and his own sung Sa still lights it.
   - Do not switch on Android's echo cancellation as the fix without first recording what it does to a sung Sa. It is likely to remove his Sa too.
10. **Show what the microphone and speaker are really doing.**
    - The mic check shows the settings the tablet actually applied (sample rate, echo cancellation, noise suppression, auto-gain) and the tablet's media volume, and warns if Bluetooth is connected.
    - Log how clear each voiced moment was, so the clarity cut-off (0.82, line 310) can be loosened if his breathy "aa" keeps falling below it.
11. **Before Stage 2:** move the listening into a background audio thread (AudioWorklet), checking every 10 ms, so the echo game is quick and smooth.
12. **Fade the feedback over time:**
    - Stage 1: a glow on every success.
    - Stage 2: about 2 in 3.
    - Stage 3: only at the end of a step.
    - Then mostly off.
    - Check skills with the screen face-down while the app keeps listening and logging quietly.
13. **Keep private records for you:**
    - each try's pitch trace, with replay
    - the 3-tap log
    - "Sang it on his own" entries
    - the sound before any over-excitement or distress, and when he used "all done"
    - the monthly singing-voice rating
    - an export for the teacher
14. **Keep his things safe.** Export and import the clips, his Sa setting and the log, so master copies can live off the tablet and a new tablet can be set up exactly the same.

### It should not

- show him any words, emoji, scores, counts, streaks or "level unlocked"
- ask him to sing, in voice or in text, or ask him questions
- play any "right", "wrong" or celebration sound, or make anything louder as a reward
- react to loudness in any way
- play above the ceiling, whatever the tablet's volume keys say
- keep playing after he signals "all done" or shows distress at a sound
- flash, pulse fast, or show confetti, stars or fireworks
- judge his claps (you note those in the log)
- grade him note by note while he is still singing
- move up a level, change his Sa or start a sound on its own
- play a drone with no Sa in it
- autoplay anything, or link to videos
- use any synthetic voice
- run without you starting it

## 8. Off-screen activities (the app is not the whole programme)

1. **A printed picture strip** of the six steps, with a "done" pocket. Also first-then cards ("First Three Steps, then Twinkle"), 2-card song choices, "Next: ___" cards and his own "all done" card. Paper schedules have the stronger research behind them, and they carry over to a teacher's class.
2. **A picture card for every piece,** shown while it plays: a lamp for Sa, three steps, buns, a lamb or a kite, a rain cloud, a star for the Bilawal song. This was the main adaptation used by the teachers at Hitham Trust in Chennai.
3. **A picture story before any change:** a new song, the singer's voice arriving, a new stage, a second Sa, a skipped day, the first visit to a teacher. Show it the day before.
4. **Copycat during play and bath:** copy any sound or movement he makes, then wait.
5. **Finish-the-line anywhere** (car, bath, bed). Use word rhymes, and sargam songs once he knows them from the recording.
6. **"Hear Sa" before bath and meals:** tap it with the screen off and hum along with it.
7. **Body music:**
   - one pat per syllable
   - a slow Keherwa march on walks, with you saying "Dha Ge Na Ti, Na Ka Dhi Na", a quiet two-finger clap on Dha and a wave on the khali
   - Dadra rocking to "Dha Dhi Na, Dha Ti Na"
   - kite glides with a scarf
   - hand heights for the notes
   - later, Keherwa on 8 floor mats
8. **The listening corner:** 5 to 10 minutes a day of real, soft, sung Bhoopali (later Yaman) at wind-down or on waking. Low volume, audio only. Let him sit beside any adult doing riyaz. Every Indian success story we found began with this kind of immersion.
9. **App-off days, twice a week from week 4:** the same routine face to face, with no tablet, or with a simple shruti box or tanpura. Judge only what you can easily judge: did he join in, did he use his singing voice rather than his talking voice, was he calm, did you both enjoy it.
10. **"Sang it on his own":** when he sings a lesson phrase later in the day, tap the button and write down the phrase. Never interrupt or correct him. This is his own practice, and probably the best real-world sign of progress.
11. **The "new adult" ladder** (from Stage 3): grandparent, then neighbour, then a video call, then a quiet visit to a teacher.
12. **Keep music time separate from YouTube rhymes.** Music time is shared play. The app has no video library and no autoplay, and screen pinning keeps him inside it.
13. **A one-page "How he learns" note for the future teacher:**
    - Give him a sung model to copy.
    - Signal his turn with a gesture, not words.
    - He may echo your questions back instead of answering.
    - He copies exactly, so sing plain, in-tune notes, softly.
    - Clap quietly near him; loud, sudden sounds can upset him.
    - He has an "all done" card. Please always honour it.
    - His Sa is ___ and stays fixed for pieces he knows. New pieces at other Sa's are being introduced slowly.
    - Singing the lesson hours later at home is his practice.

## 10. Risks, what to watch for, and when to get help

**What to watch for**
- **More "wound-up" taps since starting.** Check the "sound just before" notes. Go back a step, lower the volume, and drop the newest sound for a week.
- **Signs a sound is upsetting him:** covering his ears, flinching, crying, leaving. Follow section 6, rule 15. If it keeps happening, tell the audiologist and the OT.
- **Him using "all done" often.** Read what came just before. Shorten sessions, lower the volume, or drop the newest thing. Never stop honouring the card.
- **Upset at a changed song, key or voice.** This is why Sa is locked for known pieces, takes are fixed, borrowed tunes are used only in his own key, only one thing changes at a time, and every change is previewed with a picture card.
- **Getting stuck on one Sa.** The second-Sa step in Stages 3 and 4 is there so a class or exam doesn't come as a shock. Agree the exam Sa well in advance.
- **The screen pulling him in:** asking for the tablet, staring at it, wanting videos. Keep more steps face-down, keep the diya small, add app-off days, count his YouTube time in the daily total, and use screen pinning.
- **You running out of steam.** That is the most common way home programmes end. Use the minimum day without guilt, read your "what got in the way" notes, and simplify rather than stop.
- **The app build slipping.** Use the no-app version of weeks 1 and 2, and build in the order in section 9. Nothing later is needed early.
- **No singer found, or the singer becomes unavailable.** The harmonium base carries on and nothing is lost. The first year's material is recorded in one session, and a backup singer can record new material if needed. Keep asking, including online teachers.
- **Losing the recordings** to a broken tablet, cleared app data or a reinstall. Keep master copies off the tablet from the recording day.
- **The app misjudging him:** crediting other voices, your voice during his gap, or the drone, or missing his soft, breathy singing. Until the week-4 fixes in section 9 are in, don't trust the old Level 1 log counts, and stay silent during his windows.
- **Voice strain** (a hoarse or tired voice). Stay between Sa and Pa, keep holds short, offer "mmm", and never play "hold it as long as you can".
- **Pace creeping up toward exams.** The 12-month goal in section 4 is the honest target.
- **Too little structure for him.** The "never ask" approach comes from advice about children who avoid demands, and many autistic children enjoy clear structure. If he does better with more, make the strip and the ring cue more prominent, still without spoken commands.
- **Phrases being echoed all day.** Keep adult words to a few short lines that are worth hearing echoed ("Again", "All done", "Next: snack").
- **Privacy.** Keep recordings of him and of the singer on the tablet, plus one private backup you control (an encrypted family drive or a family PC). Get the singer's written consent. Share only with a teacher you choose.

**What is a design guess, not research**

These are reasonable starting points to adjust from his log:
- the hello tune
- using Western nursery tunes as sargam songs, and the new tunes such as the Kite song
- the ±75-cent start and the 50 to 60 cent wobble limit
- the 55 to 60 dB(A) target
- the quiet-clap default
- "match his energy, then slow down"
- the echo-scoring thresholds
- playing his own voice back to him
- the timing of the second-Sa step
- the drone-subtraction filter, if it is ever used

Review the log every 4 weeks and change whatever doesn't suit him.

**When to get a hearing test (an audiologist)**
- Soon, and before fine-tuning volume or the glow: a hearing test and a check of his sound tolerance. Your paediatrician can refer you. Start the plan while you wait.
- Again if his reactions to sound change, or he often shows distress at sounds.

**When to bring in a music therapist**
- If after about 6 to 8 weeks he rarely takes turns, or sessions often end wound-up or upset.
- If you would like coaching. Three to six online sessions for you mirror the professionally coached parents in the MAP study.
- For help choosing his Sa, checking the recordings, or preparing for a teacher trial. The Music Therapy Trust in Delhi is one Indian option.

**When to bring in a speech and language therapist**
- For any question about his talking, understanding or echolalia. This programme is not speech therapy and should not replace it.
- If he already has a speech therapist, share this plan so the cues match: gestures for turns, and short first-person phrases.

**When to bring in an occupational therapist**
- For a sensory profile, to tell sensation-seeking from sound-sensitivity before fine-tuning volume, the glow and the "big moment".

**When to see your paediatrician**
- If he loses words or skills he had.
- If his distress at sounds starts to limit everyday life.
- If his voice stays hoarse.

**And always**
- If he consistently doesn't enjoy it, pause. Shared enjoyment is the point.
