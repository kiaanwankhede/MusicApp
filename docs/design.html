2. What the evidence says

Promising, not conclusive

Reviews find music-based intervention may help social, communication and behavioural skills in young autistic children, but evidence is mostly small, low-to-moderate quality (Griffith review, Springer 2025).

Sensory care matters

A qualitative study of music groups found play that respects sensory sensitivities, promotes calm and gives structured interaction was associated with more interactive behaviour (small, descriptive).

Therapist-led works best

Most evidence is for music therapy with a therapist. An app should complement, not replace, a certified music therapist and the child's therapy team. Don't make clinical claims.

3. The opportunity

No clear product combines (a) a progressive, skill-building path, (b) sensory-safe design, (c) zero-failure interaction suited to age 4, and (d) a parent co-play mode with coaching. That is the niche.

4. Design principles

1 · Sensory-first

Volume cap, soft instrument timbres, no sudden loud sounds, muted colours, optional reduced motion, per-sound on/off.

2 · Errorless learning

No wrong notes, no buzzers, no scores. Only the next key is enabled, so a wrong tap is impossible. Prompts fade gradually (full guide → partial → none).

3 · Predictable

Same layout and routine each session: Hello song → Play → Choose → Goodbye song. Visual schedule + "all done" always visible.

4 · Follow the child's interest

Child picks instrument/theme (trains, animals…). Supports repetition and stimming-friendly loops without discouraging them.

5 · Short, flexible sessions

3–8 minute sessions with a gentle visual timer. Ends on success, never mid-frustration.

6 · Parent in the loop

Co-play mode (turn-taking, call-and-response), simple coaching tips, a private progress view, exportable for therapists. Child-safe: no ads, no purchases, no chat, locked parent area.

7. MVP scope & next steps

MVP (iPad first)

Home, visual routine, drum, colour keys, sensory settings, simple progress. Offline, no accounts, no ads. iPad is a good first target (large screen, common in autism families).

Build

SwiftUI or Flutter; pre-recorded soft samples (low latency); local storage only; COPPA / GDPR-K aligned privacy.

Validate

Start with your son (short, relaxed sessions, note what he enjoys). Then talk to a board-certified music therapist and OT/SLP for feedback; try 3–5 other families before wider release.

This is a design concept, not medical advice. It is not a substitute for therapy; consult your child's care team.
