What the research found

Ten findings, and how sure we are of each

1Strong

Music learning, not therapy

Reviews and the largest trial (364 children aged 4–7) found no clear gain in social or speech skills from music therapy. Aim for real skill and shared fun.

6Weak–moderate

A real voice; note names always sung

Children match a voice better than an instrument. He should never hear "Sa Re Ga" spoken: he may store it as speech. A low-risk precaution.

2Moderate

You don't need to be a singer

Parents with no training ran simple music activities well. What families kept doing was enjoying it together. Plan for your worst day.

7Moderate

Small range, shape first, one fixed Sa

About D4 to A4 suits ages 3–5: Sa up to Pa. Autistic people often match a tune's shape better than its exact pitch. He may remember exact keys.

3Moderate

Copy him first

When adults copied each sound, autistic 3- to 5-year-olds made more sounds and took more turns. Parents learned it quickly.

8Moderate

Loud sound needs care

Most parents of autistic children report sound sensitivity. Soft starts, a real volume limit, a Calm button and a hearing test. (Calming methods: weak evidence.)

4Moderate

Pause before the word he knows

Stop just before a predictable word and wait 3–5 seconds; any sound counts. Not yet tested inside songs: there it rests on practice experience.

9Strong

The first exam is realistic around 7

The Pune Gandharva school gives 7 as the minimum age for Prarambhik. Your local centre's rule still needs checking. A teacher trial fits around 5.

5Moderate

Rhymes first; Sa is a warm-up, not a gate

Indian classes for under-6s start with songs and rhymes, not with holding Sa. Holding one note is not reliably the easiest task.

10Strong

The old test app rewarded the wrong thing

It added up in-tune moments, folded the octave, heard men's voices, showed him text, and its volume limit wasn't real. Version 2 fixes this.

The approach in one picture

He leads, you copy, the music carries on

The order stays the one a guru teaches in: Sa with the tanpura → Sa-Pa-Sa → Sa Re Ga Pa → slow alankars → a sargam song → Raag Bhoopali. He enters each step through what he already does well.

Week 0 · 3 to 7 days

Set-up · about 60–90 minutes of your time; nothing new for him

  1. Install the new app. Bluetooth off, screen pinning on (it keeps the tablet inside the app), run the mic check.
  2. Set the volume ceiling with him in the room. Play the loudest sound; lower it until, at his seat, it is clearly softer than your talking voice (about 55–60 dB(A) on a sound-meter app).
  3. Find his Sa, without asking him. When he sings a rhyme on his own, tap "Listen to him", on 3 different days. Steady and between C#4 and F4: use it. Otherwise D4 (safed 2). Then Sa lock.
  4. Book one singer, and a backup, for one recording session in week 2 (week 3 at the latest).
  5. Print the 6-card strip with a "done" pocket, "Next: snack / bath / play" cards, his "all done" card, the song picture cards.
  6. Pick his best 5 minutes: not hungry, not tired, not straight after YouTube.
  7. Ask your paediatrician about a hearing test. Start the plan while you wait.

Week 1 · days 1 to 7 · 4–6 minutes

"Awaaz": sound play

No recordings needed yet. He sits with you, makes any sounds he likes, pats along, maybe fills in a word.

  • Hello: the diya's wick appears first ("sound is coming"), then the harmonium plays the hello tune. You don't need to sing.
  • Copycat (screen down): copy his sound a little softer, then wait 3–5 s. If he's quiet about 20 s, one soft rising-and-falling "whoooo".
  • Lamp, free sing (screen up, no drone): a higher voice lifts the diya higher. No target, no reward. Watch him, not the screen.
  • Song: his favourite rhyme in its usual words, one pat per syllable. From day 2, stop before the last word and wait up to 5 s.
  • Laya, then the quiet ending: three lamps go out, the goodbye tune, "All done", "Next: snack".
Special daysDays 3, 5, 6: calm trials in the quiet ending: (1) tanpura alone, (2) you hum one long soft "mmm", (3) six slow pats. Note which settles him. Day 4: app-off day, same routine face to face. Day 7: rest or a minimum day.

Week 2 · days 8 to 14 · 5–6 minutes

"Sa is home"

The same six steps, with a longer Lamp (60–90 s) in three parts:

  1. Sing together: harmonium Sa with the tanpura; you hum softly along. The diya just glows: it is not listening.
  2. Two listen-and-wait turns: Sa fades, the diya's ring opens, you show an open palm, everyone waits 5 s. If he sings, the diya follows him. Nothing is scored.
  3. Kite glides: he slides his voice on "whoo"; each glide ends with one soft Sa, so every glide comes home.
  • Song adds "Three Steps" on the harmonium: Sa Re Ga, Ga Re Sa. Your hands show the heights: tummy, chest, chin.
  • Hear Sa before bath and meals: tap, hum along. Never hum Sa without it.
  • Listening corner at wind-down: 5–10 min of soft sung Bhoopali aalap, audio only.
  • The singer records this week or next. Day 11: app-off day. Day 14: pin his best calm option.

After the first month · one stage at a time

Stages 1 and 2 · about months 1 to 6

Stage 1 · "Sa is home"

about months 1–3

He enjoys the routine, takes sound turns with you and sings the missing note in familiar sargam songs.

What you do

  • Copycat first, every day; then 2-note echoes: Sa-Sa, Sa-Re-Sa.
  • Sing together on Sa, then up to 3 listened Sa turns.
  • Three songs, each with its own picture card: Three Steps, the Ga Re Sa song (or the Kite song), and a third in month 2. Only Sa to Pa, always ending on Sa.
  • Gaps grow: the final Sa first, then other line endings.
Move on whenOn 3 calm days he fills the last gap with a sung (not spoken) sound in most tries, in two songs, at least once with the tablet face-down. Being in tune is not needed.

App: plays her song line by line, stops, opens the ring, waits 5 s. Any voice carries the song on.

Stage 2 · "Echo me, echo you"

about months 3–6

He echoes 2- to 4-note phrases with the right shape (up, down, same), and makes up tiny tunes of his own.

What you do

  • 3 copycat turns he leads, then 3 phrases from the singer: Ga-Re-Sa, Ga-Pa-Ga, Sa-Pa-Sa.
  • Hand signs for heights: Sa tummy, Re chest, Ga chin, Pa top of the head.
  • Dadra (6 beats), a very soft tabla theka, and later real claps, each as one week's new sound.
Move on whenRight shape on most short phrases on 3 days (one with the screen down), two sargam songs sung whole with the recording, calm over the theka.

App: judges the whole reply from his own first note, so his key doesn't matter.

3 · Sargam song and slow alankars

about months 6–12, age 4½ to 5
  • A 4-line sargam song in Bhoopali notes, one line a week.
  • Slow 3-note alankars, one note a second.
  • "Which song is this?" from its first 3 notes.
  • New phrases at a second Sa, slowly; known songs stay at his Sa.

Move on when: the whole sargam song on 3 calm days; two alankars with the right shape; he has sung with a new familiar adult there.

4 · Raag Bhoopali, teacher trial

about age 5 to 6
  • Bhoopali up and down as far as his range allows.
  • Alankars slow, then medium; Teentaal.
  • New adults step by step: grandparent, neighbour, a video call, then a quiet visit.

Move on when: the teacher judges he can learn new material from them, and he is calm in their space.

5 · Ready for class

about age 6 to 7
  • Practise at the agreed exam Sa for a few months.
  • Sing with tabla; show the taals with claps and waves.
  • A mock audition at home; quiet visits to the centre; a picture story of exam day.

Ready when: the teacher recommends him, and he sings calmly for a new adult in a new room.

Ages are rough guides, not deadlines. The full aaroh-avaroh and the whole raag are 2- to 3-year goals. Slow singing does not mean slow learning.

Sound and calm

Soft, predictable, and easy to stop

1One volume ceiling, kept by the tablet. Set with the loudest sound. Each session the app sets the tablet's volume and ignores the volume keys.
2The drone sits 6–10 dB below the loudest sound.
3Nothing starts suddenly. The wick appears 1 second before any sound; everything fades in and fades out.
4No beeps, cheers, applause or "wrong" sounds. Success is never louder: the music simply goes on.
5One new sound a week, at 30–50% of the ceiling. Louder only if the log stays calm.
6Your sounds count too: quiet claps (two fingers on your palm), a soft voice, no sudden "Yay!".
7The ending is always the quietest part. End before he tires.
8The screen: dark and warm, one small diya by your face, a glow that eases and never flashes. No confetti, stars or fireworks. Loudness never changes what he sees.
Wound up (excited)"Copy him, but smaller": softer and slower each time, nothing new on screen. Not settled in about a minute? Hold Calm.
Upset by a soundCovers ears, flinches, cries, goes still, moves away: hold Calm. Everything fades within about a second. End that step. That sound is left out for a week, then comes back softly.
The big momentSoft two-handed pats on beat 1, then always back down: pats, finger-taps, a silent wave, hands on lap.

Where the voice comes from

The singer: one real voice for the first year

Who to ask

  • A female relative or neighbour, or an older girl who sings (a boy only if under about 11: his voice may soon break).
  • A senior Gandharva or music-college student, a school music teacher, a temple or bhajan singer.
  • An online classical teacher, who records at home.

A steady, plain voice matters more than fame. A small fee is normal. She signs a one-page consent for family use. One still photo for the "Didi sings" card, not a video.

Line up a backupSomeone who could record new material if she moves away. If used, her voice is that week's one new sound; songs he knows keep the first singer's takes.

How she records

  • One session of 60–90 minutes with breaks, in week 2 or 3.
  • At his Sa, not her own, with the app's backing track in one earphone.
  • Soft, plain, straight notes: no slides, shakes or vibrato. About one note a second.

About 60 clips

  • "Saaa" and "mmm" on Sa; Re, Ga, Pa held.
  • Short phrases, in sargam and on "aa".
  • Hello and goodbye with his name; the first songs; a sung "Again" and "All done".
  • Later material, kept until needed.

Each take is kept as the one he hears. Master copies live off the tablet.

The app will check each clip (planned, week 3)More than 30 cents (a third of a key) off his Sa: ask for a retake. Never corrected note by note.

For you

After music time, and the grown-ups corner

After every session: 3 taps, 20 seconds

  • How was he? calm / OK / wound-up
  • Did we both enjoy it? yes / a bit / no
  • Did he take turns? none / 1–3 / 4 or more
  • If it applies: he used "all done", a sound upset him (which one), something got in the way, a short note.

Grown-ups corner (hold 2 s)

Set-up checklist · programme · volume ceiling · his Sa · session options · mic check · your private tuner (with earphones, out of his earshot) · singer and backing tracks · log and export.

"He sang it on his own"A button on the home screen. When he sings a lesson phrase later in the day, note it. Never interrupt or correct him: it is his own practice, and the best sign of progress.

A realistic 12-month goal (about age 5)
  • A light singing voice between Sa and Pa.
  • Sa within about one harmonium key on most days.
  • 2- to 4-note echoes with the right shape.
  • 3 to 5 sargam songs.
  • Pats and claps roughly in time with you.

If he hears more than he can yet sing, that is normal. Missed days cost nothing; a 2-minute day counts.

Month 1 going well looks likeCalm sessions you both enjoy, a few sound turns, maybe a word filled in. Nothing has to be in tune yet.
When to get help
  • Audiologist: soon, for hearing and sound tolerance; again if his reactions to sound change.
  • Music therapist: if after 6–8 weeks he rarely takes turns or often ends upset, or for coaching.
  • Speech therapist: any question about talking or echolalia. This is not speech therapy.
  • Occupational therapist: a sensory profile.
  • Paediatrician: if he loses words or skills, or his voice stays hoarse.

If he consistently doesn't enjoy it, pause. Shared enjoyment is the point.

Design guesses, not researchThe hello tune, the new tunes such as the Kite song, the ±75-cent start for the first Sa turn, the 55–60 dB(A) target, the quiet-clap default, "match his energy, then slow down", and the timing of the second Sa. Start there and adjust from his log every 4 weeks.
