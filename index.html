Programme

Week 0: set-up. Week 1 "Awaaz" (sound play). Week 2 "Sa is home". Week 3 "Didi sings" and week 4 "Laya enters" need the singer's recordings: they are coming in a later update. Until then, keep doing week 2.

Volume ceiling (set with him in the room)

"Play the loudest sound" plays the loudest thing music time ever plays: the harmonium Sa with the tanpura (the drone) underneath, at full level. Everything else is softer. Lower it until, at his seat, it sounds clearly softer than your normal talking voice (about 55–60 dB(A) on a phone sound-meter app, if you have one).

Bluetooth audio is connected — turn Bluetooth off for music time.

Set the tablet's media volume by hand to the step you marked before each session (YouTube shares it).

Fine trim (app level)Only the app's own sound, 30–100 %. Once a ceiling is saved, music time and Hear Sa use it, not this slider.

His Sa (home note)

Sa is the home note that his music always comes back to. One Sa is used for everything he knows: harmonium, tanpura, hello tune, Three Steps, the singer's recordings and the backing tracks. Default: D (White 2, safed 2) = 293.66 Hz. Results below are a note plus or minus "cents": 100 cents = one harmonium key, 50 cents = half a key.

Find his Sa

When he starts singing a rhyme on his own, quietly tap "Listen to him" and let it run 30–60 seconds (the screen goes dark; tap Stop at the bottom to finish). Do it on at least 3 different days. It never asks him to sing.

"Listen to the video" is a hint only — the music under the singer confuses it. Do this during his normal YouTube time, not before music time.

Microphone sensitivity

Microphone check

Hum or sing yourself, then stay quiet. It should name the note while you hum and say "Quiet" while you are quiet. This check listens to the wide range (70–1100 Hz, so it hears grown-ups too); in music time the app only listens in a child's range (180–1000 Hz).

Bluetooth audio is connected — turn Bluetooth off for music time.
 

Your private tuner

For you only: with earphones, out of his earshot. It plays Sa (harmonium and tanpura) in your earphones and shows how close your hum is. Two minutes a day is plenty: hum along with Sa, then Sa-Pa-Sa, then Ga Re Sa. Earphones matter: without them the tuner hears the harmonium, not you. "Cents" measure how far off you are: 100 cents = one harmonium key; within about 12 cents is right on.

Singer & recordings

In week 3 one real singer's voice becomes his model for note names, songs and echo phrases. Book her now (week 0), and line up a backup. She records about a year's material in one session in week 2 or 3.

Who to ask (any one person with a steady, plain voice)

  • a female relative or neighbour; an older girl who sings, or a boy under about 11 (a boy's voice can break within months)
  • a senior Gandharva or music-college student, a school or college music teacher, a temple or bhajan singer
  • an online classical teacher, who records at home and sends the files back

A small fee is normal. She signs a one-page consent for family use. Take one still photo of her for the "Didi sings" card (a photo, not a video).

How she records

  • At HIS Sa, not her own. She listens to the backing track below in one earphone while she sings.
  • Soft, plain, straight notes: no meend (slides), gamak (shakes), vibrato or concert-style emotion. About one note a second.
  • The hello and goodbye are sung over the app's own hello track, so they line up with what he heard in weeks 1 and 2.
  • Each take is kept as the one he hears. Keep the master copies off the tablet (a private family drive or PC).

Backing tracks for the singer

Made at his Sa as 16-bit mono WAV files (22050 Hz): (1) tanpura drone, 90 s; (2) hello and goodbye tune over the tanpura; (3) Sa reference: harmonium Sa held with the tanpura, 30 s; (4) Three Steps over the tanpura; plus (5) clip-list.txt with the clip list and his Sa as note, Hz and harmonium key. Send them by WhatsApp if she can't come in person.
